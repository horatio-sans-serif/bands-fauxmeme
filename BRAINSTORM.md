## 1. The name, in layers

**Layer 0 — phoneme.** A phoneme is the smallest unit of sound that changes the meaning of a word.
It means nothing by itself. Its only job is to make two words different.
Example: *pat* and *bat* differ by one phoneme, /p/ versus /b/. Linguists call a pair like that a
**minimal pair**.

**Layer 1 — meme.** Built on Layer 0's idea of a smallest unit. Richard Dawkins coined *meme* in
*The Selfish Gene* (1976) as the smallest unit of culture that gets copied from person to person. He
shortened it from the Greek *mīmēma*, "a thing imitated", so that it would rhyme with *gene*.
Example: a catchphrase, a reaction image, a dance move.

**Layer 2 — fauxmeme.** Built on Layer 1. *Faux* is French for "false", and it is pronounced "foe".
A meme is already an imitation (Layer 1), so a fauxmeme is a fake imitation: something that has the
shape of shared culture but was never shared. Nobody started it, no group of people owns it, and it
spreads because it *looks like* something everyone already knows.
Example: an AI-generated character that arrives with a catchphrase, fan art and an "origin" all at
once, on the same day.

**Layer 3 — the name is itself a minimal pair.** Built on Layers 0 and 2. Said aloud:

```
phoneme    /ˈfoʊ.niːm/
fauxmeme   /ˈfoʊ.miːm/
```

The two words differ by exactly one phoneme: /n/ becomes /m/. Both are nasal consonants, so the ear
barely notices the swap. So the name does what it describes: it is a fake word that passes for a
real one because only the smallest possible unit was changed. That parallels Meat Proxy §00, where
the name assigns a role to the reader. Here the name runs a test on the listener — did you hear the
difference?

## 2. How it sits next to Meat Proxy

```
 MEAT PROXY                          FAUXMEME
 the relay (a person)   ──carries──▶  the payload (a unit of fake culture)
 real documents, real wear            perfect copies with no original
 "Borrow the language.                "Invent every word, and make it
  Never invent it."  (Rule 02)         sound borrowed."
 degradation done by a machine,       degradation done by a filter,
  "no step simulated" (§08)            every step simulated
```

Meat Proxy is about the person in the middle of the route. FAUXMEME is about what travels along the
route: the thing that gets forwarded without anyone checking where it came from. Each project's
rulebook is the other's rulebook turned inside out, which is the main reason to keep them as two
projects rather than fold one into the other.

## 3. Five readings, ranked

Ranked by how much they share with Meat Proxy's existing machinery (form system, build, charter)
and how cheaply each can be tested.

1. **A band that exists only as its own memes (recommended to explore first).** FAUXMEME never plays.
   The work is a body of convincing evidence that it did: crowd videos of shows that never happened,
   screenshots of a fandom, lyric "misheard" threads for songs that were never recorded. The
   audience's belief is the instrument. Cheapest test: make three artefacts and see whether a
   stranger asks where to hear the band.
2. **A sound project built from phoneme-sized pieces.** Take the short audio stings everyone
   recognises from feeds and cut them down to phoneme length, so they keep their familiarity but
   lose their meaning. Then build new "stings" that sound recognisable and are not. This reading
   uses the phoneme half of the name most literally, and it could run through `audio/speeker`.
3. **The payload layer of Meat Proxy.** A companion record or zine: the requests, forwards and
   catchphrases that pass through the proxy, each one catalogued as a fauxmeme. A section of MP-0000,
   not a project of its own.
4. **A minimal-pair test of real versus fake culture.** Pairs of artefacts, one real meme and one
   invented one that differs by a single unit. The viewer picks which is real. This is the only
   reading with a number at the end (the share of people who guess right), so it is the easiest to
   assess.
5. **A dictionary of fauxmemes.** Invented slang with invented etymologies, typeset as a real
   reference book. The closest to Borges's *Tlön* (see §5), and the furthest from a band.

|---|---|
| 02 Borrow the language. Never invent it. | Invent every word. Make each one sound already said. |

## 5. Canon to read against

1. **Jorge Luis Borges, "Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius" (1940).** A fake encyclopedia entry slowly
   turns into the real world. The closest existing work to the idea.
2. **Jean Baudrillard, *Simulacra and Simulation* (1981).** A simulacrum is a copy with no original.
   A fauxmeme is a simulacrum at the size of one joke.
3. **Slender Man (2009).** Invented on the Something Awful forums as a photo-editing contest entry,
   then treated as real folklore by people who never saw the thread. A fauxmeme that became a meme.
4. **Fake bands that became real: Spinal Tap, Gorillaz, The Residents.** Each one shows a different
   way fabricated lore turns into a real audience.
5. **AI "brainrot" characters (2025).** Characters generated with a name, a voice and a catchphrase
   already attached, spreading as if they had a history. The present-day version, and the
   pathology Meat Proxy's Rule 04 would point at.
